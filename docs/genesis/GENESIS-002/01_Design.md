# Project Identity - Design

## Background

As Aevon evolves over many years, its implementation will change, but its identity must remain stable. A formal Project Identity document ensures that the platform's purpose, philosophy, and long-term direction remain consistent regardless of changes in technology, contributors, or AI models.

---

# Problem Statement

Without a documented identity:

- Future contributors may misunderstand the platform's purpose.
- Architectural decisions may become inconsistent.
- AI systems assisting development may lose long-term context.
- The project may drift away from its founding vision.

---

# Objectives

The Project Identity shall:

- Define who Aevon is.
- Define why Aevon exists.
- Preserve the founder's vision.
- Establish long-term engineering philosophy.
- Provide a stable reference for all future architectural decisions.

---

# Design Goals

The identity should be:

- Timeless
- Human-readable
- AI-readable
- Technology-independent
- Easy to evolve through governance

---

# Design Principles

- Clarity over complexity.
- Principles over implementation.
- Knowledge before code.
- Long-term thinking.
- Human-governed evolution.

---

# Constraints

- Identity should not depend on a programming language.
- Identity should not reference temporary technologies.
- Identity should remain valid across future generations.

---

# Success Criteria

The design is successful if:

- New contributors immediately understand Aevon.
- Future AI systems can reason about Aevon's purpose.
- Architectural decisions remain aligned with the identity.
- The identity remains stable for decades.

---

# Expected Benefits

- Consistent engineering decisions.
- Reduced architectural drift.
- Better onboarding.
- Stronger governance.
- Long-term sustainability.
