# Purpose

This document establishes the fundamental principles that govern Artificial Intelligence engineering within the AEVON Platform.

These principles provide the philosophical, ethical, architectural, and engineering foundation upon which every AI capability shall be designed, developed, validated, deployed, operated, and continuously improved.

They ensure consistency across Human Engineers, AI Engineers, FORGE, and all future intelligent systems.

---

# Scope

These principles apply to:

- AI Engineering
- Large Language Models (LLMs)
- AI Agents
- Multi-Agent Systems
- Prompt Engineering
- Context Engineering
- Memory Systems
- AI Automation
- Decision Support Systems
- Knowledge Engineering
- Autonomous Workflows

---

# Vision

To establish Artificial Intelligence as a trusted engineering collaborator that amplifies human capability while remaining governed by engineering discipline, transparency, and accountability.

---

# Mission

The mission of AI within AEVON is to:

- Accelerate engineering.
- Improve decision quality.
- Reduce repetitive work.
- Preserve organizational knowledge.
- Enable intelligent automation.
- Support innovation.
- Enhance engineering excellence.

AI shall assist engineering—not replace engineering judgment.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

Artificial Intelligence is an engineering capability.

It shall never be treated as:

- A shortcut
- A replacement for engineering
- An unquestionable authority
- An uncontrolled automation engine

Every AI capability must demonstrate measurable engineering value.

---

# Core Principles

---

## Principle 1 — Human-Centric Engineering

Human engineers remain responsible for:

- Engineering decisions
- Architecture approval
- Security approval
- Production approval
- Ethical considerations

AI augments engineering capability but does not assume engineering accountability.

---

## Principle 2 — Engineering Before Intelligence

AI solutions shall always be engineered before they are optimized.

Priority order:

1. Correctness
2. Reliability
3. Maintainability
4. Explainability
5. Intelligence
6. Performance

Engineering quality takes precedence over model sophistication.

---

## Principle 3 — Context Before Generation

AI shall receive sufficient context before generating outputs.

Context may include:

- Requirements
- Architecture
- Standards
- Source Code
- Documentation
- Historical Decisions
- Organizational Knowledge

Incomplete context increases engineering risk.

---

## Principle 4 — Memory Before Repetition

Knowledge shall be preserved.

Previously solved engineering problems should not be solved repeatedly.

AI systems shall maximize knowledge reuse through:

- Memory
- Knowledge Bases
- Templates
- Engineering Patterns
- Prompt Libraries

---

## Principle 5 — Explainability

Every significant AI output shall be explainable.

Engineers shall understand:

- Why an answer was produced.
- Which context influenced the result.
- What assumptions were made.
- Which engineering standards were applied.

Opaque reasoning shall not be accepted for critical engineering decisions.

---

## Principle 6 — Traceability

Every AI interaction shall be traceable.

Traceability records should include:

- Prompt Version
- Context Version
- Model Version
- Generated Output
- Human Reviewer
- Approval Status
- Timestamp

Engineering history shall remain auditable.

---

## Principle 7 — Governance

AI shall operate within approved engineering governance.

Governance includes:

- Security Policies
- Engineering Standards
- Architecture Standards
- Quality Gates
- Approval Workflows
- Compliance Requirements

AI shall never bypass governance mechanisms.

---

## Principle 8 — Security by Design

AI systems shall be designed with security as a foundational requirement.

Engineering activities shall address:

- Prompt Injection
- Data Leakage
- Unauthorized Access
- Sensitive Information
- Model Abuse
- API Security

Security is integrated into the engineering lifecycle.

---

## Principle 9 — Continuous Validation

Every AI output shall be validated.

Validation methods include:

- Human Review
- Automated Testing
- Standards Verification
- Architecture Compliance
- Security Review
- Quality Assurance

No AI-generated artifact shall bypass validation.

---

## Principle 10 — Continuous Learning

Engineering knowledge shall continuously evolve.

Learning sources include:

- Production Experience
- Lessons Learned
- Metrics
- User Feedback
- Incident Reviews
- AI Performance
- Engineering Retrospectives

Learning shall strengthen future engineering outcomes.

---

# Supporting Principles

The following supporting principles reinforce the core philosophy.

## Reuse Before Reinvention

Prefer:

- Existing Components
- Existing Prompts
- Existing Agents
- Existing Templates
- Existing Knowledge

Engineering duplication shall be minimized.

---

## Simplicity

Solutions should be as simple as possible while satisfying engineering objectives.

Complexity requires explicit justification.

---

## Modularity

AI capabilities shall be modular.

Modules should be independently:

- Developed
- Tested
- Replaced
- Improved
- Reused

---

## Scalability

AI systems shall support future growth without requiring fundamental redesign.

---

## Observability

Every AI workflow shall expose operational visibility through:

- Logs
- Metrics
- Traces
- Performance Data
- Quality Indicators

Observability enables engineering confidence.

---

## Reliability

AI systems shall prioritize:

- Predictable behavior
- Stable outputs
- Graceful degradation
- Fault tolerance
- Recovery mechanisms

# Decision Hierarchy

When principles conflict, engineering decisions shall follow this order:

1. Human Safety
2. Security
3. Engineering Integrity
4. Legal & Compliance
5. Architecture
6. Quality
7. Maintainability
8. Performance
9. Productivity
10. Convenience

This hierarchy ensures consistent engineering priorities.
