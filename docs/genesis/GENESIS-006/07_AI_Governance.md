# Purpose

This document establishes the AI Governance Framework for the AEVON Platform.

AI Governance defines the policies, controls, responsibilities, approval mechanisms, operational guardrails, and accountability model required to ensure that Artificial Intelligence remains trustworthy, secure, explainable, compliant, and aligned with AEVON's engineering vision.

Governance enables innovation while ensuring that AI operates within clearly defined engineering boundaries.

---

# Scope

This framework applies to:

- AI Models
- AI Agents
- FORGE
- Prompt Engineering
- Context Engineering
- AI Memory
- Knowledge Systems
- Autonomous Workflows
- AI-Assisted Engineering
- Future Intelligent Services

---

# Vision

To establish a governance model where Artificial Intelligence operates as a trusted engineering partner under transparent policies, measurable controls, and continuous human oversight.

---

# Governance Philosophy

Artificial Intelligence is governed engineering.

AI shall never operate outside approved engineering policies.

Every AI capability must be:

- Explainable
- Accountable
- Auditable
- Secure
- Traceable
- Measurable
- Continuously Reviewed

Governance exists to enable safe innovation—not restrict progress.

---

# Governance Objectives

The framework shall:

- Protect engineering integrity.
- Preserve human accountability.
- Ensure regulatory compliance.
- Prevent uncontrolled AI behavior.
- Improve engineering consistency.
- Protect organizational knowledge.
- Support responsible automation.
- Enable continuous governance.

---

- Human Oversight

# Governance Levels

## Level 1 — Advisory

AI provides recommendations only.

Human approval is mandatory.

---

## Level 2 — Assisted

AI performs engineering tasks under supervision.

Outputs require review before implementation.

---

## Level 3 — Controlled Automation

AI executes predefined workflows.

Exceptions require human approval.

---

## Level 4 — Autonomous Execution

AI performs approved operational activities independently within defined governance boundaries.

Critical engineering decisions remain under human authority.

# Human Accountability

Human Engineers remain responsible for:

- Engineering Decisions
- Architectural Approval
- Production Deployment
- Security Approval
- Regulatory Compliance
- Risk Acceptance

AI may recommend but shall not replace accountable engineering leadership.

# Risk Management

AI risks include:

- Hallucinations
- Prompt Injection
- Data Leakage
- Unauthorized Actions
- Bias
- Knowledge Drift
- Model Degradation
- Operational Failures

Each risk shall have defined mitigation strategies.

# Explainability

Production AI systems shall provide:

- Decision Summary
- Supporting Context
- Applied Standards
- Confidence Score
- Source References
- Validation Status

Critical engineering decisions shall never be opaque.

---

- Context Version
- Memory Version
- Agent Version

- User
- Timestamp
- Decision Path
- Approval Status

Complete traceability shall be maintained.
