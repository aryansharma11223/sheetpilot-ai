# Purpose

This document establishes the foundational principles that govern the creation, management, sharing, protection, evolution, and utilization of knowledge within the AEVON Platform.

These principles provide the philosophical and operational foundation for all knowledge-related engineering activities and ensure that knowledge remains a trusted, reusable, and continuously improving organizational asset.

---

# Scope

These principles apply to:

- Engineering Knowledge
- AI Knowledge
- Business Knowledge
- Project Knowledge
- Operational Knowledge
- Organizational Knowledge
- AI Memory
- Knowledge Graphs
- FORGE
- AI Agents
- Future Knowledge Services

---

# Vision

To establish a knowledge-driven engineering culture where information is transformed into validated organizational intelligence that supports every engineering decision throughout the lifecycle of the AEVON Platform.

---

# Philosophy

Knowledge is one of the organization's most valuable assets.

Unlike physical assets, knowledge increases in value when it is:

- Shared
- Validated
- Connected
- Reused
- Improved

Knowledge shall be engineered with the same discipline applied to software, infrastructure, and architecture.

---

# Knowledge Principles

## Principle 1 — Knowledge is an Engineering Asset

Knowledge shall be treated as a managed engineering asset.

It shall have:

- Ownership
- Lifecycle
- Version
- Governance
- Quality
- Security
- Traceability

---

## Principle 2 — Single Source of Truth

Every engineering concept shall have one authoritative source.

Duplicate knowledge shall be minimized.

Conflicting knowledge shall be resolved through governance.

---

## Principle 3 — Knowledge Before Documentation

Documentation is a representation of knowledge.

Knowledge shall exist independently of any individual document.

Multiple documents may reference the same knowledge object.

---

## Principle 4 — Knowledge Must Be Structured

Knowledge shall never exist as unstructured information alone.

Every knowledge object shall include:

- Metadata
- Classification
- Relationships
- Version
- Owner
- Status

---

## Principle 5 — Knowledge Must Be Discoverable

Knowledge that cannot be found has little practical value.

Every knowledge object shall support:

- Keyword Search
- Metadata Search
- Semantic Search
- Vector Search
- Graph Navigation

---

## Principle 6 — Knowledge Must Be Connected

Knowledge gains value through relationships.

Every knowledge object shall identify its relationships with:

- Requirements
- Architecture
- Standards
- Projects
- AI Memory
- Decisions
- Risks
- Lessons Learned

---

## Principle 7 — Knowledge Must Be Reusable

Knowledge shall be created once and reused whenever applicable.

Reuse reduces:

- Engineering effort
- Cost
- Risk
- Inconsistency

---

## Principle 8 — Knowledge Must Be Governed

Every knowledge object shall have:

- Owner
- Reviewer
- Approval Status
- Review Cycle
- Security Classification
- Audit Trail

---

## Principle 9 — Knowledge Must Be Trusted

Only validated knowledge shall become organizational knowledge.

Trust requires:

- Verification
- Review
- Traceability
- Evidence

---

## Principle 10 — Knowledge Evolves

Knowledge is never static.

Knowledge shall evolve through:

- Engineering feedback
- Lessons Learned
- Innovation
- AI recommendations
- Continuous improvement

---

## Principle 11 — Knowledge is Contextual

Knowledge has meaning within context.

AI systems shall retrieve:

- Relevant knowledge
- Current knowledge
- Authorized knowledge
- Task-specific knowledge

Context determines relevance.

---

## Principle 12 — Knowledge Enables Intelligence

Artificial Intelligence does not replace knowledge.

AI amplifies the value of well-structured knowledge.

The quality of AI reasoning depends directly upon the quality of available knowledge.

Knowledge shall be:

- Honest
- Accurate
- Transparent
- Explainable
- Secure
- Respectful of intellectual property
- Free from intentional manipulation

Ethical knowledge management strengthens organizational trust.
