# Purpose

This document establishes the Enterprise Verification Principles for the AEVON Platform.

Verification confirms that every engineering artifact conforms to approved requirements, architectural standards, engineering specifications, regulatory obligations, and organizational policies.

These principles define the foundation for consistent, objective, measurable, and repeatable verification across all engineering disciplines.

---

# Scope

These principles apply to:

- Business Requirements
- Enterprise Architecture
- Software Design
- Source Code
- AI Systems
- Infrastructure
- Platform Services
- Security Controls
- Documentation
- Operational Procedures

---

# Vision

To establish a verification culture in which quality is engineered into every activity through disciplined review, objective evidence, and continuous validation of engineering outputs.

---

# Verification Philosophy

Verification shall be:

- Objective
- Evidence-Based
- Independent
- Repeatable
- Risk-Oriented
- Standards-Driven
- Automated where practical
- Continuously Performed

Verification is an engineering discipline rather than a final project activity.

---

# Core Principles

## Principle 1 — Verify Every Requirement

Every approved requirement shall have at least one verification method.

Verification methods include:

- Inspection
- Analysis
- Demonstration
- Testing

No requirement shall remain unverified.

---

## Principle 2 — Early Verification

Verification begins during:

- Business Analysis
- Architecture Design
- Technical Design

Defects discovered early have significantly lower remediation costs.

---

## Principle 3 — Independent Verification

Critical engineering artifacts shall undergo independent verification.

Independent reviewers shall not verify their own work where organizationally practical.

---

## Principle 4 — Evidence-Based Decisions

Verification conclusions shall rely upon documented evidence.

Examples include:

- Review Records
- Test Results
- Static Analysis Reports
- Security Reports
- Performance Reports
- Audit Logs

Opinions alone shall not constitute verification.

---

## Principle 5 — Standards Compliance

Every engineering artifact shall be evaluated against:

- Enterprise Standards
- Architectural Standards
- Coding Standards
- Security Policies
- Regulatory Requirements
- Industry Best Practices

Compliance shall be measurable.

---

## Principle 6 — Risk-Based Verification

Verification effort shall correspond to risk.

Higher-risk systems require:

- Additional Reviews
- Increased Test Coverage
- Security Validation
- Performance Verification
- Operational Readiness Assessment

---

## Principle 7 — Automation First

Verification activities should be automated whenever practical.

Automation includes:

- Static Analysis
- Unit Testing
- Integration Testing
- Security Scanning
- Compliance Validation
- Infrastructure Validation

Manual verification remains essential where human judgment is required.

---

## Principle 8 — Traceability

Every verification activity shall be traceable to:

- Requirements
- Design
- Source Code
- Test Cases
- Deployment Records
- Operational Metrics

Traceability supports governance and auditability.

---

## Principle 9 — Continuous Verification

Verification shall continue throughout the software lifecycle.

Continuous verification includes:

- Continuous Integration
- Continuous Testing
- Runtime Validation
- Operational Monitoring
- Security Monitoring

Verification does not end after deployment.

---

## Principle 10 — Continuous Improvement

Verification practices shall evolve through:

- Lessons Learned
- Incident Reviews
- Audit Findings
- Quality Metrics
- Technology Advancements

Continuous learning strengthens engineering maturity.

---

# Verification Methods

The enterprise shall recognize four primary verification methods:

| Method | Purpose |
|---------|----------|
| Inspection | Manual examination of engineering artifacts |
| Analysis | Mathematical, logical, or engineering evaluation |
| Demonstration | Functional proof under controlled conditions |
| Testing | Objective execution against expected outcomes |

Complex systems may require multiple verification methods.

---

# Verification Levels

Verification shall occur at multiple levels:

- Requirements Verification
- Architecture Verification
- Design Verification
- Component Verification
- System Verification
- Integration Verification
- Operational Verification
- Production Verification

Each level contributes to overall system quality.

---

# Verification Evidence

Acceptable verification evidence includes:

- Review Checklists
- Test Reports
- Validation Reports
- Security Assessments
- Performance Benchmarks
- Compliance Reports
- Deployment Records
- Operational Dashboards

Evidence shall be retained according to enterprise record retention policies.

---

# Verification Independence

Verification independence shall follow four levels:

| Level | Reviewer |
|--------|----------|
| Level 1 | Self Verification |
| Level 2 | Peer Verification |
| Level 3 | Independent Team Verification |
| Level 4 | External or Regulatory Verification |

The required level shall depend on system criticality.

---

# Governance

Verification governance shall ensure:

- Consistent Methodology
- Qualified Reviewers
- Standardized Checklists
- Documented Evidence
- Audit Readiness
- Continuous Oversight

Governance promotes repeatable engineering quality.
